03 / About me

Curious by nature.
Grounded in making.

I’m a Bachelor of Architecture student at the University of Arkansas School of Architecture and Design, graduating in 2027.

I believe architecture carries a responsibility to improve the built environment for future generations. My work explores how careful observation, clear ideas, and hands-on testing can lead to spaces that feel generous and connected to their surroundings.

At the University of Arkansas Laser and Model Lab, I help students bring ideas into the world through laser cutting, shop tools, and 3D printing. I’m happiest learning alongside others — in the studio, in the shop, or outside with a sketchbook.

03

Talks + events

Lectures, conversations, and visits that bring new ideas back to the studio.

Modus PK19 · First year

At Modus Studio’s PK19 event, I connected with people shaping our region through music advocacy, downtown Fayetteville revitalization, the Ozark Natural Science Center, and cycling.

The conversations showed how community-driven design begins with listening. Architecture is connected to the lives and stories a place holds, and the evening offered perspectives I could bring back into my studio work.

Modus PK20 · Following year

I returned to Modus Studio’s fabrication shop for PK20, an evening connecting the creative community through a wide range of ideas.

Highlights included Tim Ernst’s waterfall photography and humor, Jason Quall’s inventive creations such as a robotic giraffe on a tricycle, Willa Thomason and Alex Brown’s insights on sustainability in landscape and urban forestry, and Emma Gastineau’s perspective on caves.

The event was a reminder of how much there is to learn beyond the studio—and dogs were welcome, too.

Hay Festival · Dallas City Hall

I attended a Hay Festival conversation with Mark Lamster, Stephanie Drenka, and Veletta Lill at the historic Texas Theatre in Oak Cliff. The discussion focused on saving Dallas City Hall, historic preservation, and the relationship between architecture, government, and the people it serves.

As an architecture student, I was interested in how preserving City Hall means more than saving a building. Its location and organization support communication between departments and provide a central place for citizens to engage with their government. The conversation highlighted the social and civic value of the buildings we already have.

It was my first visit to the Texas Theatre. Exploring its gathering spaces showed how a historic building can continue to bring people together through events and informal conversations. I also picked up a signed copy of Mark Lamster’s Welcome to Paradox City.

Fay Jones lectures

I have had the privilege of attending lectures at the Fay Jones School of Architecture and Design at the University of Arkansas as part of my architectural education.

This Spring 2024 Lectures + Events schedule represents one season of the school's programming. Attending the lecture series gives me opportunities to hear perspectives from beyond the studio and reflect on them alongside my own work.
